**Per-realm SCIM provisioning monitors** (8, one per realm with a `scim-client`) plus a fix for a permanent phantom diff on the Keycloak dashboard. Follows #53, which added the SCIM dashboard section; these are the monitors that page rather than relying on someone looking at it. The first version was a single aggregate monitor, and the page it produced read `SCIM provisioning failing — IdP token rejected (401)` with **no indication of which tenant** — near-useless on call, because every SCIM realm belongs to a different agency whose own IdP admins have to make the fix, so the tenant is the single most important field in the alert. It also understated the problem: with per-realm monitors it is immediately visible that **three** tenants are broken (`ed`, `faa`, `ntsb`), not one. **Why `for_each` and not `group_by`:** a multi-alert monitor cannot be built here — the realm lives in raw URL text inside an istio-ingressgateway access log, and `group_by` on `@realm`, `@http.url_details.path` and `@http.status_code` all return nothing (verified 2026-09-09). The only groupable fields are `host`, `service` and `kube_namespace`, all uniform across these logs. **Threshold is `> 0` over a 1d window, and both halves are measured rather than assumed.** `> 0` breaks the repo's prefer-rates-over-counts rule deliberately: a correctly configured IdP never receives a 401 here, so zero is the only correct steady state and there is no baseline to express as a rate. It is also what the data requires — 401s per hour over 30 days show only 12 non-empty hours out of 720, distributed `{1 → 6 hours, 2 → 3, 3 → 1, 7 → 1, 20 → 1}`, so most broken hours contain just one or two 401s and the `>= 3 in 1h` rule originally considered would have fired in 3 of 12 hours, missing 75% of the outage. Volume makes it safe rather than noisy: 42 real-IdP 401s in 30 days total, so it physically cannot flood — the opposite failure from PR #42's count monitor. The 1d window exists to stop flapping: IdP retries are sparse and irregular (ED: 14:46, 14:51, 15:00, 15:14, 15:34, then nothing until 16:54), and with a 1h window the monitor would recover between retries and re-alert on each one, with every fresh Alert transition paging regardless of `renotify_interval`. `on_missing_data = "default"` because no SCIM requests at all is normal (12 active hours in 30 days) and must not be treated as breaching. The **fan-out is accepted here and it is not the #49/#50 situation**: 8 monitors on a signal totalling 42 events in 30 days, re-notifying daily, versus 214 groups across 25 orgs re-firing every 2h. A fleet-wide break pages 8 times, which is arguably correct since 8 different agencies must act, and it buys per-tenant muting and routing an aggregate monitor cannot. `doc`, `sss`, `ncua` and `opm` have never sent a SCIM request; their monitors sit in OK via `on_missing_data` at no cost, but mean a newly-wired IdP is covered from its first request rather than after the next outage. **Not done, deliberately:** a log pipeline Grokking these access logs into real `realm` and `status_code` attributes would collapse this to one multi-alert monitor *and* replace the fragile `401` text match with `@status_code:401`. A Grok parser cannot be verified before shipping without a pipeline test API, and a wrong one silently stops parsing — which would recreate exactly the silent-green failure this whole effort exists to fix. The org already runs 6 pipelines (two for Keycloak) so the pattern exists; it is written up in `monitors.tf` as the follow-up. Also adds `"notify_list": null` to `dashboards/aigov/keycloak-full.json`: Datadog always returns that key while the repo JSON omitted it, so `terraform plan` reported "1 to change" on the dashboard **forever**, applying nothing. A permanent phantom diff trains people to ignore plan output, so it is worth closing.
